## One-line

File-level claim/release/state/broadcast exposed as MCP tools over a CRDT-merged shared filesystem;
finds that coordination rather than parallelism or the merge substrate is what produces the gain.

## Why it's in the library

Only one of the three coordination papers whose license lets us keep the bytes. Also the one with
real frontier models in the loop, and the source of the result that naive parallel-merge and
sequential hand-off both score *below* a single agent working alone.

**Careful with:** the claim protocol is an *advisory* lock enforced at the prompt layer, deliberately.
A summary that says the room "refuses conflicting writes" without saying "advisory" inverts the thesis.
Cells are small (n=6 to n=32).
