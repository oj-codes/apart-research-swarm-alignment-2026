## One-line

Treats concurrent change as a pre-write *admission* problem: workers declare a versioned ChangeIntent
against an exact base commit, and a deterministic control plane admits compatible intents and fails
closed on ambiguous authority.

## Why it's in the library

The dissenting position among the three — deterministic and enforced where the others are advisory.
Gives us the vocabulary for a distinction this team keeps running into: an **integration conflict** is
detected after changes exist; an **authority conflict** exists earlier, when two workers hold
incompatible rights or premises. Its line that "git worktrees and branches isolate bytes during
generation, but they do not establish that two planned changes are compatible" is the formal version
of why our worktrees stop live collisions but not merge conflicts.

**Careful with:** six CooperBench task pairs, reported "only as feasibility evidence" and
"intentionally too small for comparative claims." There is no comparative performance result in this
paper. Don't cite it for one.
